Who provides requirements at each level
Initiative IN
Business Business defines the initiative — what market problem are we solving, what is the measurable outcome, what is the time horizon. This is the "why" of the product.
Problem: [What gap in the market are we addressing?]
Outcome: [Measurable goal, e.g. 50 farmers onboarded in 6 months]
Audience: [Who benefits? Growers, B2B buyers, B2C buyers?]
Constraints: [Budget, timeline, compliance, geography]
Feature FT
Business + Product Business describes the capability needed (what users should be able to do). Product/tech confirms feasibility and shapes the feature boundary. This is the "what" at a high level.
Feature name: [Short descriptive name]
User need: [As a [role], I need to be able to [capability]]
Business value: [Why does this matter? Revenue, retention, compliance?]
Out of scope: [What this feature does NOT include]
Success metric: [How will we know this feature is working?]
Epic EP
Business + Product Business defines the functional area and the rules that govern it. Tech breaks it into buildable chunks. For Harvest Market, business must provide the rules — e.g. commission %, listing windows, GST treatment, dispute resolution policy.
Epic name: [Functional area]
Business rules: [Any specific rates, windows, thresholds, or policies]
Australian compliance: [GST, ABN, BAS, state-specific rules]
Roles involved: [Which user types interact with this epic]
Priority: [MVL / Phase 2 / Nice to have]
User story HM
Business validates Tech writes Tech writes the story and acceptance criteria. Business reviews and approves the criteria — confirming the story matches their intent before dev starts. Business should reject stories where the acceptance criteria don't match the business rule.
As a [role], I want to [action] so that [outcome]

Acceptance criteria:
- [Specific, testable condition 1]
- [Specific, testable condition 2]
- [Edge case or error handling]

Business rule reference: [Link to confluence/doc or inline rule]
Where business requirements are missing right now
Dispute resolution policy — what happens step by step when a buyer raises a dispute? Who decides? Time limits?
Refund policy — full refund, partial refund, or credit? Who absorbs the loss — platform or farmer?
Grower onboarding criteria — what must a grower prove to be approved? ABN only or food safety certs, insurance?
Delivery zones & fees — which postcodes are served? Flat fee or distance-based? Minimum order for delivery?
Market calendar — will the Wed window ever change? How far in advance? Who can change it?
Commission tiers — is 10% flat for all farmers forever? Any volume discounts or tiered rates?
Buyer credit terms — do B2B buyers ever get invoice terms (pay later)? 30-day accounts?
Product standards — are there minimum quality standards? What happens to non-compliant products?

Where in Jira business adds requirements
Initiative Add to: Jira Goals (Premium) or Confluence page linked to epic ▶

Jira Free doesn't have an Initiative issue type. Use a Confluence page titled "Initiative: [name]" and link it to all related epics. Or create an Epic with the label initiative as a workaround. Business owns creating and updating this document — it's the source of truth for why work exists.

Feature Add to: Epic description field in Jira ▶

In Jira Free, Features sit at the Epic level. Business fills in the Epic's description field with the feature template (user need, business value, out of scope, success metric). This becomes the brief that tech uses to write stories. Business should update this before the epic is pulled into a sprint.

Epic Add to: Epic description + custom fields in Jira ▶

Business adds business rules to the epic description (rates, windows, compliance requirements). In Jira, create a custom field called "Business rules" on the Epic issue type. Tech should not start writing stories until business has filled this in. Examples for Harvest Market: commission rate, dispute window hours, listing window days/times.

User story Add to: Story description + comments in Jira ▶

Tech writes the story and acceptance criteria. Business reviews by commenting in Jira with approval or change requests. Use the Jira workflow: To Do → In Review (biz) → Approved → In Progress. Business should only comment on whether the acceptance criteria correctly capture the business intent — not implementation details.
